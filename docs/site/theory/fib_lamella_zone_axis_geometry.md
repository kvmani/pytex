# FIB Lamella Geometry for a Target Zone Axis

This note derives the geometry implemented in `pytex.fib` and exposed in the workbench as
**EBSD → FIB lamella**: given the orientation of a grain measured by EBSD on a bulk surface, which
vertically milled lamella shows a chosen zone axis in the TEM, at what azimuth it must be cut, what
residual tilt the TEM holder must then supply, and how sure that number is when the lamella's
rotation on the grid is not controlled.

Symbols follow {doc}`../standards/terminology_and_symbol_registry` (section *FIB lamella
planning*). The specification, with the maintainer's frozen decisions D1–D9, is
`docs/architecture/fib_lamella_planning_foundation.md`; the workbench walk-through is
{doc}`../workflows/fib_lamella_planning`, and the instrument protocol that makes the FIB azimuth
trustworthy is {doc}`../workflows/fib_azimuth_calibration`.

## 1. The governing constraint

A lamella milled **strictly vertically** — the ion beam travelling along the inward surface normal
— has its two large faces perpendicular to the surface. Its plane normal $\mathbf{n}_{L}$ therefore
lies *in* the surface plane, and in the TEM the electron beam enters along $\mathbf{n}_{L}$. The set
of beam directions a vertical lamella can offer is the great circle of in-plane directions of the
sample frame, and nothing else.

A grain is usable for the zone axis $\langle uvw \rangle$ exactly when some symmetry equivalent of it
lies close to that great circle, and the angular distance of that equivalent from the circle **is**
the tilt the holder has to supply. Everything below follows from this.

```{figure} ../../figures/fib_lamella_frames.svg
:alt: The sample frame, the vertical lamella with its normal in the surface, the ion beam along minus Z, the target zone axis at epsilon star out of the surface, and the SEM image, FIB ion view and TEM holder frames.
:width: 96%

The frames of FIB lamella planning, generated by `scripts/generate_fib_lamella_figures.py` from a
lamella solved by `pytex.fib.lamella_geometry`. $\mathbf{n}_{L}$ lies in the surface at azimuth
$\theta_{S}$; the target $\mathbf{d}^{*}$ rises $\varepsilon^{*}$ out of it.
```

## 2. Frames

Five frames carry the answer from the EBSD map to the microscope; PyTex registers each through
`pytex.core.frames` rather than inventing a private model.

| Frame | Definition | Built by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| crystal $C$ | the phase's crystal frame | `pytex.core.lattice` |
| sample $S$ | $X_s, Y_s$ in the surface, $Z_s$ the **outward** normal | `SurfaceGeometry` from the EBSD specimen frame |
| SEM image $I$ | pixel columns, pixel rows | `ImageRegistration` |
| FIB ion view $P$ | plan view at stage tilt $T$ | `ChamberGeometry` |
| lamella $L$ | $\mathbf{n}_{L}, \mathbf{t}_{L}, Z_s$ | the solve of section 3 |
| TEM holder $H$ | $x$ along the rod, $z$ the beam at zero tilt | `pytex.tem.reconstruction.HOLDER_FRAME` |

**The sample frame is declared, not assumed.** EBSD vendors do not agree on whether the specimen
$z$ axis points into or out of the surface, nor on which way the scan rows run, and PyTex's scan
readers import vendor angles without remapping (decision D1 also fixes that the file already carries
the 70° tilt correction). `SurfaceGeometry(normal_sign, scan_y_sign)` states both. With
$X_s = \hat{x}_E$ and $Z_s = s_n \hat{z}_E$, completing the right-handed set forces
$Y_s = Z_s \times X_s = s_n \hat{y}_E$, so the specimen-to-sample rotation is

$$
\mathbf{M} = \operatorname{diag}(1, s_n, s_n), \qquad \det \mathbf{M} = +1,
$$

and a scan coordinate $y$ maps to $Y_s$ with the factor $s_n s_y$.

## 3. The out-of-plane angle and the lamella frame

PyTex orientations map crystal to specimen, $\mathbf{v}_{E} = \mathbf{g}\,\mathbf{v}_{C}$
({class}`pytex.core.orientation.Orientation`). The foundation document wrote the next step for the
opposite convention and asked for it to be verified; with PyTex's convention the sample-frame
image of a unit crystal direction $\mathbf{u}$ is

$$
\mathbf{d}_{S}(\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{g}\,\mathbf{u}.
$$

`tests/unit/test_fib_geometry.py` pins this with a triclinic crystal and
$\mathbf{g} = R_x(30^{\circ}) R_z(40^{\circ})$: $\mathbf{g}[100]$ rises
$\arcsin(\sin 40^{\circ} \sin 30^{\circ}) = 18.75^{\circ}$ out of the surface while
$\mathbf{g}^{\mathsf{T}}[100]$ lies in it, so the transposed reading fails the test.

**Orbit (decision D8).** Any symmetry equivalent is acceptable, in either sense:

$$
U = \{\, \mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{u}_{C} : \mathbf{s}_{i} \in G \,\} \cup \{\, -\mathbf{u} : \mathbf{u} \in U \,\}.
$$

Member indices are computed exactly as $\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{D}\,[uvw]$ with
$\mathbf{D}$ the direct-basis matrix, an integer matrix for every operator of a point group in its
own lattice.

**Out-of-plane angle and best member.**

$$
\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}) = \arcsin \lvert \mathbf{d}_{S}(\mathbf{u}) \cdot Z_s \rvert, \qquad
\mathbf{u}^{*} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{u} \in U} \varepsilon(\mathbf{u}), \qquad
\varepsilon^{*} = \varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{*}).
$$

**Lamella frame.** With $\mathbf{d}^{*} = \mathbf{d}_{S}(\mathbf{u}^{*})$,

$$
\mathbf{n}_{L} = \frac{\mathbf{d}^{*} - (\mathbf{d}^{*}\cdot Z_s) Z_s}{\lVert \mathbf{d}^{*} - (\mathbf{d}^{*}\cdot Z_s) Z_s \rVert}, \qquad
\mathbf{t}_{L} = Z_s \times \mathbf{n}_{L}, \qquad
\theta_{S} = \operatorname{atan2}(\mathbf{n}_{L}\cdot Y_s,\, \mathbf{n}_{L}\cdot X_s).
$$

$(\mathbf{n}_{L}, \mathbf{t}_{L}, Z_s)$ is right-handed because
$\mathbf{n}_{L} \times (Z_s \times \mathbf{n}_{L}) = Z_s$. In it the chosen member sits at
$(\cos\varepsilon^{*}, 0, \sigma\sin\varepsilon^{*})$ with $\sigma = \operatorname{sign}(\mathbf{d}^{*}\cdot Z_s)$: it has no
component along the long axis, which is why the section schematic of the workbench can draw it in
the plane of the page.

**Canonical choices.** A plane normal and its reverse describe the same lamella, so
$\theta_{S} \in [0^{\circ}, 180^{\circ})$. Among members tied in $\varepsilon$ the smallest canonical azimuth is taken,
then the member along $+\mathbf{n}_{L}$, then the one rising along $+Z_s$. These rules depend on the
*set* of options only, so $\varepsilon^{*}$, $\mathbf{n}_{L}$ and $\mathbf{t}_{L}$ are invariant under any symmetry
operator applied to $\mathbf{g}$ — a property the tests check operator by operator.

The whole of this section is vectorized over orientations and orbit members with `einsum`; the
preparability raster of a map is one call.

## 4. Where the lamella goes: the footprint

The azimuth is fixed by the crystal, so the only freedom left is translation. The grain mask is
resampled, nearest-neighbour, onto a grid aligned with $(\mathbf{t}_{L}, \mathbf{n}_{L})$; a summed-area table
answers "does an $a \times b$ window lie wholly inside the grain" for every position at once; the
clearance $m$ is the largest margin for which the rectangle $(L + 2m) \times (W + 2m)$ still fits,
found by bisection because fitting is monotone in $m$; and among the positions achieving it the one
deepest inside the grain (by the Euclidean distance transform) is taken. When even $L \times W$ does
not fit, the longest lamella of width $W$ that does is reported. The result is resolved to the map
step and uses NumPy and SciPy only.

## 5. The unknown mounting rotation

There is no deterministic lift-out convention (decision D6). Once welded to the grid the lamella's
in-plane rotation $\phi$ about $\mathbf{n}_{L}$, and whether it was turned over, are unknown until it is
looked at. The residual $\varepsilon^{*}$ is a known *angle*; its split into holder tilts is not.

The mounting hypothesis $(\phi, s)$ sends $\mathbf{n}_{L}$ to $s\,\hat{z}_{H}$ and $\mathbf{t}_{L}$ to
$(\cos\phi, \sin\phi, 0)$. On the front branch ($s = +1$) the chosen member arrives at

$$
\mathbf{w} = (-\sigma\sin\phi\sin\varepsilon^{*},\; \sigma\cos\phi\sin\varepsilon^{*},\; \cos\varepsilon^{*}),
$$

and matching it to the double-tilt beam direction $(-\cos\alpha\sin\beta, \sin\alpha, \cos\alpha\cos\beta)$ of
{doc}`tem_specimen_tilt_navigation` gives the exact tilts

$$
\alpha = \arcsin(\sigma\cos\phi\sin\varepsilon^{*}), \qquad \tan\beta = \sigma\sin\phi\tan\varepsilon^{*}.
$$

For small angles $\alpha \approx \varepsilon^{*}\cos\phi$ and $\beta \approx \varepsilon^{*}\sin\phi$ — a picture of the shape
of the feasible arcs, never the computation. On the back branch the member arrives at exactly the
reverse of $\mathbf{w}$, so when both senses are acceptable the two branches coincide; they differ
only for one-sense (polarity, CBED) work, where the flip decides success.

A plan reports three things and never conflates them:

1. **Guaranteed** — the exact tilt for $\varepsilon^{*} + U + m$, with $U$ the expanded uncertainty
   and $m$ the safety margin, is inside the envelope for every $\phi$ and both branches. For a
   symmetric rectangle $\lvert\alpha\rvert \le A$, $\lvert\beta\rvert \le B$ this reduces to
   $\varepsilon^{*} + U \le \min(A, B) - m$, because $\lvert\alpha\rvert, \lvert\beta\rvert \le \varepsilon$ on the curve with
   equality on the axes. For a circular envelope of radius $R$ it is *slightly less* than $R$: the
   curve bulges outside $\alpha^{2} + \beta^{2} = \varepsilon^{2}$ between the axes, which the small-angle picture
   misses.
2. **Probabilistic** — the fraction of sampled $\phi$ for which *some* orbit member is inside the
   envelope. Each $\phi$ is solved with {func}`pytex.tem.navigation.plan_tilt_to_zone_axis`, the
   validated TEM solver; map-wide ranking evaluates the same four exact branches of
   {func}`pytex.tem.navigation.solve_tilts_for_direction` vectorized, and the two are pinned to
   agree.
3. **Unreachable** — no $\phi$ works.

A plan never reports one $(\alpha, \beta)$ pair as if $\phi$ were known.

## 6. The uncertainty budget

Four standard uncertainties are combined in quadrature with unit sensitivity (JCGM 100:2008):

$$
u(\varepsilon^{*}) = \sqrt{u_{\mathrm{EBSD}}^{2} + u_{\mathrm{GOS}}^{2} + u_{\mathrm{reg}}^{2} + u_{\mathrm{mount}}^{2}}, \qquad
U = k\,u(\varepsilon^{*}).
$$

Unit sensitivity is an upper bound: an orientation error of $\delta$ moves the target by at most
$\delta$, and an azimuth error $\delta\theta$ changes the residual by at most $\delta\theta$ (exactly, the
angle becomes $\arccos(\cos\varepsilon^{*}\cos\delta\theta)$). The registration term is the affine
control-point misfit divided by the RMS radius of the points, the angular error it implies for an
azimuth read through the registration. The verdict of section 5 is judged on $\varepsilon^{*} + U$, and
a plan feasible only at the point estimate is flagged.

## 7. The FIB azimuth is calibrated, never asserted

At the column angle $T$ the ion view is the undistorted plan view and the SEM view is foreshortened
by $\cos T$ across the tilt axis. The pattern rotation is

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{ion}} = s_{R}\,(\theta_{S} + R_{\mathrm{st}}) + R_{0},
$$

and $s_{R}$ and $R_{0}$ are instrument properties PyTex cannot know. They are fitted from fiducial
trenches by {func}`pytex.fib.calibrate_chamber_from_fiducials` (circular mean with period
$180^{\circ}$, because a trench has no front), and until that has been done every plan carries an
UNCALIBRATED warning. The protocol is {doc}`../workflows/fib_azimuth_calibration`.

## 8. Assumptions and failure modes

- **Columnar subsurface.** EBSD samples tens of nanometres; the lamella is micrometres deep. The
  orientation is assumed to hold over the milling depth, and every plan says so.
- **Degenerate targets.** When every member lies along $Z_s$ ($\varepsilon^{*} = 90^{\circ}$) no vertical
  lamella can view the target; the plan is flagged and its azimuth is arbitrary.
- **Vendor conventions.** A wrong `SurfaceGeometry` mirrors every azimuth; check it once per
  instrument against a fiducial.
- **Grid topology.** Placement needs a square-grid scan with a declared step; hexagonal scans are
  refused with the reason.
- **Validation status.** The chain is validated analytically and against the TEM solver, not yet
  against a lamella cut on a real instrument with its achieved tilt recorded.

## 9. Validation

There is no MTEX equivalent of this workflow, so MTEX parity is not available as the floor, as
`docs/testing/strategy.md` states explicitly. The substitute lanes are
analytic ($\varepsilon = 0$, $90^{\circ}$ and a constructed $12.5^{\circ}$ to $10^{-9}$), orientation convention,
symmetry invariance, frame round trip, registration, the TEM-solver cross-check ($\varepsilon^{*}$
recomputed by `plan_tilt_to_zone_axis` for the mounted lamella), the envelope arcs against their
closed form on an asymmetric rectangle, the footprint against a hand-computed rectangle, golden
prose, and the workbench panel. The executable worked examples are in {doc}`../examples/index`
(section *FIB lamella planning*).

## 10. Novelty classification

- **Established methods** — the orientation-to-sample transform, symmetry orbits, angle to a plane,
  zone-axis tilt solving, inscribed-rectangle placement.
- **Useful engineering improvements** — the convention-explicit EBSD → FIB → TEM chain in one
  reproducible tool; map-wide preparability ranking; the printable work order.
- **Potentially original directions** — the uncertainty budget propagated into a residual tilt with
  a coverage interval, and the $\phi$-marginalized feasibility that turns an uncontrolled mounting
  rotation into a quantified fraction. Prior art for either **has not been verified**; see
  `references/reference_index.md` before claiming novelty in writing.
